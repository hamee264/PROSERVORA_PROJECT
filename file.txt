============================================================
1. PROJECT OVERVIEW
============================================================

The project is a global services marketplace that connects people
who need work with people who can provide those services.

The platform should support both digital and physical services.

Examples:

- Web Development
- Mobile Development
- UI/UX Design
- Graphic Design
- Video Editing
- Photography
- Digital Marketing
- Writing
- Data Analysis
- Tutoring
- Consulting
- Plumbing
- Electrical Services
- Cleaning
- Repairs
- Construction
- Makeup
- Other legitimate professional or local services

The platform should NOT be restricted to Nigeria or to physical
"fixing" jobs.

It should be designed from the beginning so that it can eventually
operate internationally and support different countries, currencies,
payment providers, and regulations.


============================================================
2. MAIN PROBLEM
============================================================

People often need someone to perform a specific job but have
difficulty finding a trustworthy person who can do it.

Providers also have difficulty finding customers.

Traditional methods can involve:

- Searching social media
- Asking friends
- Random online advertisements
- Unclear pricing
- Lack of payment protection
- Lack of proof of completed work
- Disputes over whether a job was completed
- Difficulty finding providers nearby
- Difficulty finding trustworthy professionals

The platform solves this by providing one structured marketplace
for discovering providers, communicating, agreeing on a price,
making protected payments, completing jobs, and handling disputes.

3. CORE MARKETPLACE MODEL
============================================================

There are two main ways a customer can find work.

A. FIND A PROVIDER

Customer searches for a service.

Example:

Customer searches:

"React developer"

The platform displays relevant providers.

The customer can:

- View provider profiles
- View ratings
- View completed jobs
- View reviews
- View services
- View portfolio
- See verification status
- See online status
- See whether the provider is available for work
- See distance/location information where appropriate
- Contact the provider
- Discuss the job
- Agree on a price
- Create a booking
- Pay through the platform


B. POST AN OPPORTUNITY

A customer can create an opportunity when they need someone
to perform a specific job.

Example:

"I need someone to build an e-commerce website."

Providers can discover the opportunity and respond.

Flow:

Customer creates opportunity
        ↓
Providers discover opportunity
        ↓
Provider responds
        ↓
Customer and provider chat
        ↓
They negotiate
        ↓
They agree on the job and price
        ↓
Booking/order is created
        ↓
Customer pays
        ↓
Job begins
        ↓
Provider completes job
        ↓
Customer confirms completion
        ↓
Provider receives payout


============================================================
4. OPPORTUNITIES
============================================================

Customers can:

- Create opportunities
- Edit opportunities
- Withdraw/cancel opportunities
- View their opportunities
- See whether an opportunity is active or taken

Opportunities do NOT have a mandatory fixed budget.

The customer and provider can agree on the final price through
the conversation/booking process.

Once an opportunity has been taken, it should no longer appear
as an available opportunity to other providers.

Opportunities can also expire after a defined period.

Current idea:

An available opportunity can disappear after approximately
2 days if it has been taken or is no longer relevant.

Important:

Deleting an opportunity from the public marketplace must NOT
delete the historical booking/order/payment records associated
with it.

Marketplace opportunity data and permanent transaction history
must be treated separately.

The provider who took an opportunity does NOT need to be publicly
displayed on the opportunity listing.

5. PROVIDERS
============================================================

A provider is a user who offers services through the platform.

A person does NOT need to create a completely separate account
to become a provider.

One account can have:

- Customer capabilities
- Provider capabilities

A customer can later become a provider.

Example:

John can hire a photographer as a customer.

John can also offer web development services as a provider.

He uses one account.

8. PROVIDER AVAILABILITY
============================================================

There are TWO separate concepts.

A. ONLINE STATUS

This indicates whether the provider is currently online.

Only:

- Online
- Offline

Online status should be automatically determined by the platform
rather than requiring the provider to manually switch between
online/offline.

B. AVAILABLE FOR WORK

This is manually controlled by the provider.

Example:

Available for work: ON

or

Available for work: OFF

These are intentionally separate.

Possible states:

Online + Available
Online + Not Available
Offline + Available
Offline + Not Available

There is NO working-day schedule requirement.

We are NOT adding:

Monday available
Tuesday unavailable
etc.

The provider simply controls whether they are currently accepting
new work.

16. PRICE NEGOTIATION
============================================================

There are no mandatory fixed prices for opportunities.

Customer and provider can communicate and agree on a price.

The agreed amount should become part of the booking/order.

IMPORTANT:

The customer should pay exactly the amount agreed by both parties.

The frontend should NOT be trusted to determine the final amount.

The backend must validate the booking amount and payment amount.

============================================================
18. PAYMENT PROTECTION
============================================================

The customer pays through the platform.

The money should not simply be transferred directly to the provider
immediately.

The platform should use a protected payment flow.

Conceptually:

Customer pays
        ↓
Payment confirmed
        ↓
Funds associated with the transaction
        ↓
Provider performs job
        ↓
Customer confirms completion
        ↓
Provider payout released

This reduces arguments about whether the provider was paid
before the job was completed.


============================================================
19. CUSTOMER COMPLETION CONFIRMATION
============================================================

The customer should be able to confirm:

"The job has been completed."

Once the customer confirms completion:

Provider payout becomes eligible/released according to the
payment provider's actual settlement/payout mechanics.

We do NOT want an unnecessary mandatory waiting period if the
customer has already confirmed successful completion.

The system should still account for legitimate payment,
settlement, fraud, and dispute constraints imposed by the
payment provider.


============================================================
20. PROVIDER COMPLAINTS / DISPUTES
============================================================

The system must NOT be one-sided.

A provider can also complain/dispute.

Example:

Customer claims:

"The provider didn't complete the job."

Provider can respond:

"The job was completed."

The provider should be able to:

- Open a dispute
- Explain the situation
- Submit evidence
- Upload relevant images/documents
- Reference relevant conversation
- Respond to customer claims


============================================================
21. CUSTOMER DISPUTES
============================================================

A customer can also dispute a job.

Example:

Provider claims:

"Job completed."

Customer disagrees.

Customer can open a dispute.

The provider should receive a notification immediately.

The system should NOT automatically refund the customer merely
because the customer clicked "dispute."

The dispute should enter a review process.

Possible result:

A. Refund customer

OR

B. Release payment to provider

OR

C. Other resolution determined by the platform/admin according
   to the evidence and rules.


============================================================
22. REFUNDS
============================================================

Refunds should be possible when legitimate problems occur.

Examples:

- Provider cannot fulfill the job
- Provider fails to complete the agreed work
- Valid dispute is resolved in customer's favor
- Payment/problem occurs

Important:

The platform's commission policy must be explicitly defined.

The current intended business model is that NaijaFix/Workora
takes a 5% commission.

However, refund behavior should be designed carefully so that
the system does not accidentally create financial inconsistencies.

Refund and commission rules must be implemented on the backend,
not calculated only in the frontend.


============================================================
23. PLATFORM COMMISSION
============================================================

Current business model:

Platform commission = 5%

Example:

Customer pays:

₦100,000

₦5,000

₦95,000

The exact payment-provider fees and who absorbs them must be
defined separately.

The commission should be configurable rather than hard-coded

============================================================
25. PROVIDER PAYOUTS
============================================================

We are NOT literally storing physical money inside our database.

The dashboard displays financial records/balances associated
with real payment infrastructure.

Payment providers such as Stripe/Paystack can handle the actual
financial movement and payout infrastructure.

Our backend records transaction states and references.

We should avoid storing sensitive raw bank/card information
ourselves when the payment provider provides secure account
connection/tokenization.

Possible stored references:

stripe_account_id
paystack_recipient_id
etc.

depending on the integration.


============================================================
26. PAYMENT PROVIDERS
============================================================

Initial payment strategy:

- Stripe
- Paystack

payment provider.

We should create a payment abstraction/service layer.

Conceptually:

Payment Service
      |
      ├── Stripe
      ├── Paystack
      └── PayPal (future)

This allows providers to be added or removed more easily.

Security is a major requirement because the platform handles:

- Personal information
- Authentication
- Messages
- Payments
- Payouts
- Identity verification
- Disputes

Security measures should include:

- Password hashing
- Secure authentication
- Proper session/token management
- Authorization
- Role/capability checks
- Input validation
- SQL injection prevention
- XSS protection
- CSRF protection where applicable
- Rate limiting
- Secure headers
- Secure cookies where applicable
- Server-side validation
- Payment webhook verification
- File upload validation
- Access control
- Audit logging
- Protection against privilege escalation

JWT is NOT automatically secure just because JWT is being used.

The implementation must be designed correctly.

39. PERFORMANCE
============================================================

The application should be designed to feel fast.

Important techniques:

- Database indexes
- Pagination
- Efficient queries
- Avoid N+1 queries
- Lazy loading
- Image optimization
- Caching where appropriate
- Optimistic UI where appropriate
- Skeleton loading
- Proper loading/error states
- Efficient API responses
- Avoid unnecessary database requests
- Background processing for heavy tasks
- CDN/object storage for media where appropriate

We specifically want to avoid the type of slow user experience
often associated with poorly optimized portals.

The goal is not merely "fast code."

The entire system should be designed around performance.

============================================================
60. PAYMENT SECURITY
============================================================

The frontend must never be trusted for financial calculations.

Example:

The customer sees:

₦100,000

But the backend determines the authoritative amount.

The backend verifies:

- Booking
- Agreed price
- Currency
- Customer
- Provider
- Payment provider
- Commission
- Payment status

Payment webhooks must also be verified.

A user must never be able to modify:

price
commission
provider
payment status
payout status

simply by editing frontend requests.

============================================================
69. PERFORMANCE PHILOSOPHY
============================================================

The application should be designed to avoid becoming slow as
the number of users grows.

Important:

- Proper database indexing
- Pagination
- Efficient queries
- Caching
- API optimization
- Image compression
- CDN/object storage
- Lazy loading
- Background jobs
- Avoid unnecessary requests
- Efficient frontend state management

Performance should be considered during architecture, not added
as an afterthought.


============================================================
70. SCALABILITY
============================================================

Although the first version may target Nigeria, the architecture
should allow the platform to expand internationally.

The system should therefore avoid assumptions such as:

- Only NGN
- Only Nigerian phone numbers
- Only Nigerian addresses
- Only one payment provider
- Only one currency
- Only physical services

The first market can be Nigeria.

The architecture should be global.
